# People First engine build

A small patch set on top of Cataclysm: DDA 0.I "Ito" (commit `27939e29b8b4ddc081490d9f51de59a459c88df6`). People First itself stays JSON-only and runs on a stock 0.I. The companion mod **People First: Engine** (`mods/PeopleFirstEngine`, id `people_first_engine`) needs this build.

## Patches (`apply_patches.py`; the full diff is in `people-first.patch` after a build)
1. **Crash safety.** `run_eocs` with an `alpha_talker`/`beta_talker` that no longer exists (left the bubble, died) runs `false_eocs` instead of running with a null actor, which segfaulted. The "no valid talker" and "Tried to use invalid npc" popups become debug.log warnings.
2. **`u_set_name` / `npc_set_name`.**
   - Syntax: `{"u_set_name": {"first": …, "last": …, "full": …, "last_from_other": true}}`. Each part is a string or variable object, and empty means keep.
   - `last_from_other` takes the other talker's surname (the last word of their name).
   - What the companion mod does with it:
     - you name your own babies, and they carry your family name;
     - an NPC couple's baby takes its mother's surname;
     - children of families spawned in the world take a parent's surname;
     - you can rename a child you take in.
3. **The "let me pass" bark** is said at most once every 15 minutes per NPC (in crowds it fired every turn); and a walk order to a tile someone else stands on ends next to it (playtest 3: an NPC waited behind the player for hours).
4. **No corpse pulping** for characters with `CANNOT_ATTACK` (children and babies).
5. **`PF_SWAP`:** walking into an NPC with this character flag swaps places instead of opening the NPC menu, where the next keypress could be "attack". People First gives the flag to everyone it tracks (hidden trait `PF_PERSON`).
6. **Standing up for the people you're bonded to.** Vanilla NPCs only defend faction mates who "watch your back", and People First's NPCs are `no_faction` lone wolves, so nobody defended anybody. `pf_protects()` adds bonded people to an NPC's friends list:
   - kin: `pf_mom`, `pf_dad`, `pf_guardian`, or the partner in `pf_sweet`;
   - the same band (`pf_band`) or household (`pf_resident`);
   - warmth `pf_v_<uid>_w` of at least the global `pf_defend_w` (default 40, the mod's "close friend").

   You count too (uid `AVATAR`). Vanilla then gives a monster within 6 tiles of a friend top priority, and its line-of-fire check stops them shooting through friends. The global `pf_set_defend = 0` (Settings → "Standing up for each other") turns it off.
7. **Follower rules only bind followers** (a vanilla bug fix). Every NPC is created with the follower rule `follow_close`, and `assess_danger` applied it to non-followers too: an NPC more than 4 tiles from the player had `must_retreat` set and ignored every monster. Now only your followers keep that rule; everyone else uses the normal 6-tile radius. This also changes vanilla NPCs, who now fight monsters near them when you're not close by.
8. **Closing in.** Once our people target distant monsters, vanilla's attack code assumed the target was near: a pistol at 25 tiles aimed forever, and a Magiclysm caster at 19 tiles spun until the infinite-loop guard fired. A People First NPC whose target is beyond the range they can use (confident range + 1) now walks toward it first.
9. **Who ran.** When one of our people (anyone with a `pf_uid`) breaks into a flat-out retreat in vanilla's `act_on_danger_assessment`, the engine stamps `pf_fled_t`, in the same units as JSON `time('now')`. JSON can't see fleeing on its own: `npc_run_away` lasts only a few turns. The mod's Reckoning judges it once things are calm.
10. **The People key.** A real input action, `people_first`, opens the mod's menu (EOC `PF_open_menu`) in one keypress. Before, it took the mutations menu plus a letter.
    - The default key is `,`, which is free in both the default keymap and the user's. It's added to `data/raw/keybindings.json` by `engine/install_data.py`, run by `build.ps1` on install; the original is kept as `keybindings.json.pf-orig`.
    - It can be rebound in the game's keybinding screen like any other action, and it's listed in the action menu under Misc.
    - The official exe ignores the unknown binding.
11. **The title screen.** The main menu shows `data/title/people_first.title` when it exists, ahead of the vanilla, alternate and seasonal arts, and prints "People First" in light cyan under the "Version: 0.I (Ito)" line. The line is skipped on a terminal too short to fit it above the menu bar.
    - The art is light blue and teal: a ring of people holding hands around a campfire under the stars, "Cataclysm: Dark Days Ahead" above, and "Nobody makes it alone" below.
    - It's generated by `engine/make_title.py` (edit that, not the `.title` file). `build.ps1` regenerates it and `install_data.py` copies it into the game's `data/title/`.
    - The official exe never reads that file, so it keeps the vanilla title.
12. **"wields a" names the NPC** (a vanilla bug fix). In `npc.cpp`, two messages passed the literal `<npcname>` tag to `add_msg_if_player_sees`, which doesn't expand tags, so the log read "<npcname> wields a hatchet." They now print the NPC's name (full run, day 61).
13. **Shots off the edge of the map** (a vanilla bug). A ranged attack's overshoot can run past the edge of the reality bubble, and `projectile_attack` raised an error screen ("Shot along (27,132,0) out-of-bounds") for each such shot. With patches 6–8 our people fight near the edge of the bubble, so it happened repeatedly (full run, day 62). The shot now just ends, and the line goes to the ballistics debug channel.
14. **Hearing the fight in the dark.** `assess_danger` skipped every monster the NPC couldn't see and kept the player as a friend only if seen, so indoors at night (or in fog) the household stood "Ignoring" a woodland wight that was blinding the player 4 tiles away (full run, days 101 and 122). Our people (those with a `pf_uid`) now count an unseen hostile within 3 tiles, or one right next to someone they protect within 12 tiles, and keep a protected person within 10 tiles (no wall between) as a friend without seeing them.
15. **Moved items lose their old owner.** An item teleported by an EOC (`npc_teleport` on an item talker) is re-added without its faction owner. The mod's housekeeping moves food back to the stores and a dead resident's things to the household; before this, a dead person's canned beans asked "considered stealing".
16. **Items answer "is it rotten?" and "is it a zombie's corpse?"** `npc_has_flag` on an item talker (the beta item in `u_map_run_item_eocs`) understands three pseudo-flags: `PF_ROTTEN` (the item, or food inside it, is past its shelf life), `PF_GOING_BAD` (90% of it) and `PF_ZOMBIE_CORPSE` (a corpse of the ZOMBIE species). JSON could read neither, so rotten cabbage stayed in the stores forever, and corpses couldn't be hauled out of the yard because one of ours might be among them (full run).
17. **One "let me pass" for everyone.** On top of patch 3's once per 15 minutes per NPC, nobody barks within 10 minutes of the last bark from anyone. With 11 people in one evac shelter (and at a band's camp) the per-person limit still meant a bark every few minutes, and the household's own lines scrolled out of the log (full run).
18. **Children run to a grown-up.** A People First child who flees (`good_escape_direction`) runs to the nearest grown-up they're bonded to (kin, household, band, close friends, you; patch 6's friends list) within 30 tiles, instead of any direction away from the threat. Full run day 151: Taunya fled a skeletal dog from 15 tiles out of home into open ground and died 58 tiles away.
19. **Running from monsters isn't running from you.** Vanilla gives a panicking non-follower `NPCATT_FLEE_TEMP`, which adds `npc_flee_player` for 24 hours: the NPC counts as an enemy and runs from the player all day. People First NPCs (with a `pf_uid`) only get the timed `npc_run_away`; one already stuck that way drops it once they stop running (unless their faction is hostile). Full run day 151: Fernando and Consuelo fled a swarm and kept running from Dante, bleeding, for hours.
20. **No flight from you unless you hurt them.** Several vanilla paths give an NPC `NPCATT_FLEE_TEMP` (an enemy that runs from the player: "A maniac northwest, near enough to see us"). A People First NPC only takes a flee attitude if you hit them (`hit_by_player`) or their faction is hostile, and one already in it drops it at once. Full run day 151: Consuelo flipped to it as Dante walked up, after patch 19. Patch 20b: the check uses the faction only (`guaranteed_hostile()` includes `is_enemy()`, true for anyone already fleeing, so the first version never cleared).
21. **A teleported NPC is there at once.** An off-screen NPC teleported into the reality bubble stayed inactive until the map shifted (missing from every scan). After teleporting an NPC to a spot inside the bubble, `game::load_npcs()` loads it. Full run day 152: Marylin Whitmore stood at home, inactive, for hours after her family arrived.
22. **Around deep water** (`npc.cpp` `get_path_avoid`): a People First NPC's path avoids deep water unless they're already standing in it. Full run day 158: Emanuel's band, heading west, stood in a river with a child; earlier a beaver in a river killed a band child. No dry route means no walk.
23. **A shared meal is eaten** (`math_parser_diag.cpp`): a math function, `u_pf_eat_share() = P`, puts P percent of the other talker (a food item) into u's stomach as eating does, so it fills you, counts in the eat menu's "Consumed today" and digests. Only `people_first_engine` calls it (`PF_ENGINE_eat_share` overrides the main mod's stub, which adds half the calories and vitamins straight to the body). Full run days 151-160: "Hungry" right after every shared meal, "Consumed today: 0 kcal". Patch 23b: reading `u_pf_eat_share()` gives how much of the item still fits in u's stomach (0-100), so a shared meal eats only what fits (day 180: "Your stomach is full to bursting").
24. **Test traces for patches 18 and 22** (`npcmove.cpp`, testing only): with the global `pf_debug_defend = 1` (Home lab "Defense: trace"), debug.log gets "PF flee" (who a fleeing child runs to) and "PF path" (each new path of one of our people, with its count of deep-water tiles). Home lab rows "Test: patch 22" and "Test: patch 18" set the tests up. Confirmed 2026-10-16: "PF path: Beau Blair … length=19 deep_water=0" (straight line 6), "PF flee: Creola Adcock friends=1 runs to=you d=8".
25. **A bigger title** (`main_menu.cpp`): the title art is centered on its own widest line (vanilla assumes 80 columns), and only the lines that fit above the version line are drawn. The People First art is 108 x 24 (35% larger than the first 80 x 18).
- **Trace (testing only):** with the global `pf_debug_defend = 1` (Home lab → "Defense: trace…"), each of our NPCs' combat decisions (friends, hostiles, target, action, weapon, distance, range) goes to `config/debug.log` as `PF defend` lines. When off, it costs one variable lookup per NPC move.

## Layout and build
- Source: `C:\pfbuild\cdda`, a shallow clone at the commit. It lives outside the project to avoid Windows' 260-character path limit in vcpkg's trees.
- vcpkg: `C:\pfbuild\vcpkg` at the latest commit. Older vcpkg can't find Visual Studio 18, and the latest uses CMake 4, which breaks the game's pinned `yasm` port. So `C:\pfbuild\overlay\yasm` (the latest yasm port) overrides it via `--overlay-ports`.
  - There's no global `vcpkg integrate install`: `build.ps1` force-imports vcpkg's MSBuild props/targets per build.
- Toolchain: Visual Studio 18 Build Tools (MSVC 14.50), Windows SDK 10.0.26100.
- `powershell -NoProfile -File engine\build.ps1` applies the patches (idempotent), builds Release x64 incrementally, saves the diff, and installs the exe as `game\cdda-0.I-2026-06-06\cataclysm-tiles-pf.exe` next to the untouched official exe. The data folder is the same commit's.
- The first build also compiles the libraries through vcpkg (about an hour); after that a patch rebuild takes minutes.

## When 0.J comes out
Re-clone at the new release commit and rerun `apply_patches.py`. It fails loudly on any anchor that moved, so each patch can be re-anchored by hand.
